\begin{abstract}
Three motions were recorded with an ICM-20948 at 500\,Hz and processed on the
Raspberry Pi Pico itself: a Butterworth low-pass, quantisation at 16, 8 and 4
bits, descriptive statistics and a 2048-point FFT. Gyroscope variance separates
the motions by six orders of magnitude and the dominant spectral component
identifies the shaking rate, while variance alone cannot distinguish walking
from shaking. The on-device transform agrees with a NumPy reference to within
0.03\,\% of the largest bin, and the full pipeline consumes 3.9\,\% of the
available sample-period budget.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
IMU, feature extraction, quantisation, FFT, embedded systems
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Method}

The selected application domain is the inertial measurement unit. The Lab 1.1
logger samples the ICM-20948 over I\textsuperscript{2}C at 500\,Hz and writes
16-byte records to an SD card. Three recordings of 15\,s were acquired: the
board at rest, carried during walking, and shaken by hand, each of 7500
samples with none dropped.

A second program reads a recording back on the Pico, converts the raw counts to
g and dps, and processes the first 2048 samples: DC removal and peak
normalisation; a second-order Butterworth low-pass designed by bilinear
transform and applied in the forward direction only; quantisation at 16, 8 and 4
bits with dequantisation and error measurement; per-axis mean, variance,
standard deviation, minimum, maximum, median and mode; and a 2048-point radix-2
FFT with a Hamming window. Each stage is timed with \texttt{time\_us\_64()}. The
analysed block and the resulting spectrum are written back as CSV, and a
host-side script recomputes the transform in NumPy over identical samples. The
Sobel and Canny operators were omitted as they are defined on images.

Two consistency checks validate the data. With the board flat the mean of $a_z$
is $-1.00048$\,g, i.e.\ gravity; the gyroscope means in the same recording,
$-0.195$, $-0.106$ and $-0.142$\,dps, are the sensor bias.

\section{Analysis}

\subsection{Feature selection}

Gyroscope X variance spans 0.0013 at rest, 127.7 during walking and 3615.9
during shaking (Table~\ref{tab:stats}). The separation approaches six orders of
magnitude and exceeds the measurement noise by a wide margin, so any threshold
between 1 and 50 distinguishes motion from rest without tuning.

Variance alone is nevertheless insufficient. The $a_x$ variances for walking and
shaking, 0.0087 and 0.0095, are effectively identical, whereas the spectra are
not: walking concentrates its energy below 1.5\,Hz, while shaking peaks at
7.57\,Hz with a first harmonic near 15\,Hz arising because a manual shake is not
sinusoidal (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Variance describes the magnitude of
motion and the dominant spectral component its rate of repetition; the two are
complementary and neither is individually sufficient.

The filter results in Table~\ref{tab:filter} are governed by the same
consideration. Walking lies inside the 5\,Hz passband and loses 2\,\% of its
spread; the still recording contains only noise, of which 27\,\% is removed;
shaking peaks above the cutoff and loses 42\,\%, taking the signal of interest
with it. The cutoff is therefore a property of the motion under investigation,
not of the sensor.

\subsection{Quantisation and numerical accuracy}

The SNR falls by 48.18\,dB between 16 and 8 bits and by 23.98\,dB between 8 and
4 bits, i.e.\ 6.02 and 6.00\,dB per bit, reproducing the expected halving of
quantisation error per additional bit.

The on-device FFT deviates from the NumPy reference by at most
$3.6\times10^{-7}$, or 0.0008\,\% of the largest bin, with all five strongest
peaks agreeing in bin index, frequency and amplitude to six decimal places
(Table~\ref{tab:fft}, Fig.~\ref{fig:shaking}). The residual is attributable to
single- against double-precision arithmetic. The relative error is largest for
the still recording only because the reference bin it is normalised against is
itself very small.

\subsection{Execution cost}

The 2048-point transform requires 160.3\,ms against the 4.096\,s needed to
acquire the same block, so processing occupies 3.9\,\% of the available time and
a single core sustains both concurrently. The per-sample cost of
78.3\,\textmu s places the theoretical ceiling near 12.8\,kHz; in practice
sensor readout and card writes reduce this, the worst observed
\texttt{f\_write} being 3.9\,ms against a 2\,ms sample period, tolerable only
because the logger buffers 4\,kB per write and samples on the second core.

The statistics dominate at 3.08\,s for six axes, almost entirely due to the
median's $O(n^2)$ insertion sort. Mean, variance, minimum and maximum are
single-pass and negligible. For $a_x$ at rest the mean is $-0.02161$ and the
median $-0.02155$, a difference of 0.3\,\%, so the median does not justify its
cost outside outlier rejection.

\section{Issues and Discussion}

Clipping occurred in two distinct forms. The first shaking recording saturated
the sensor: configured for $\pm2$\,g, samples reached 32767, the 16-bit maximum.
Such peaks are unrecoverable and the recording was repeated with a gentler
motion; the appropriate remedy is a $\pm8$ or $\pm16$\,g range, trading
resolution for headroom. The second form is implementation-side. Normalisation
places the largest sample at 0.999, which is representable at 16 bits
($32767/32768$) but not at 4 bits ($7/8 = 0.875$), where six samples fall
outside. The normalisation target should be a function of bit depth; it was
retained as the effect is instructive, though it inflates the 4-bit error
slightly.

The mode was expected to be uninformative for continuous data on the assumption
that measured values rarely repeat. The raw output is an integer count, and at
rest the signal traverses so few counts that values repeat between 103 and 777
times; in the moving recordings this falls to between 3 and 7. The mode is thus informative
only where the signal is nearly static, which is where it carries least
information.

Two improvements follow. The median should be replaced by a counting approach
over the integer counts, or dropped in favour of variance, eliminating almost
all of the 3.08\,s. Secondly, the analysis operates on a stored file rather than
a live stream, which ensured repeatable results across experiments but does not
demonstrate sustained real-time operation; executing the same code against the
live sensor is the natural continuation.
